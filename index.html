About the workshop

AI agents in the
design loop.

AI agents are beginning to participate directly in computing-system design: they reason about architectural tradeoffs, generate and optimize kernels, run experiments, and interpret simulation and physical-design feedback. These capabilities raise questions that span computer architecture, compilers, operating systems, and electronic design automation.

How should agents incorporate architectural knowledge? What tools and feedback enable effective design exploration? How can researchers establish correctness, measure improvements against strong baselines, and account for the cost of agent-driven experimentation?

Architecture 2.0 brings together researchers and practitioners to examine these questions through contributed research, invited talks, and hands-on tutorials. We focus on agents that iteratively propose, implement, test, and refine solutions, and on the architectural expertise and rigorous evaluation needed to make them useful.

Toward the CCC Visioning Workshop

This HPCA event is a community-building primer for an approved Computing Community Consortium (CCC) Visioning Workshop on this topic, to be held at ISCA in June 2027. Contributed talks, tutorial experience, and the closing discussion will feed directly into the CCC agenda. Participation at HPCA is open to everyone, whether or not you attend the CCC workshop.

Software

Hardware-aware GPU kernel optimization

Explore agent-guided optimization of a selected GPU kernel with cloud GPU resources and inference credits. See validation, profiling, and trajectory analysis, and learn how tool use and run-to-run variation affect results.

Presenter TBA

Architecture

Benchmarks and reasoning

Evaluate an LLM on a small public subset of QuArch, then examine ArchEval's evaluation settings, simulator use, and baseline-normalized scores. Learn to separate reasoning failures from tool-use and evaluation failures.

Presenter TBA

EDA

Macro placement

Use a simple circuit to explore how visual feedback can guide macro placement. Reasoning traces and placement animations show how a vision-language model and an analytical placer interact, including quality, constraints, and failure modes.

Contribute

Call for papers

Submissions open Dec 1, 2026

We invite research on how AI can advance the design, analysis, optimization, and evaluation of computing systems. We welcome early results, completed research, and perspectives that identify important problems.

Topics of interest

Architecture

  • AI-driven microarchitecture and accelerator design
  • Design-space exploration
  • RTL generation and physical design
  • Memory and interconnects
  • Performance, power, and reliability modeling

Systems

  • Kernel generation and tuning
  • Compiler, runtime, and OS optimization
  • Resource management
  • Bottleneck diagnosis
  • Hardware–software co-design

Infrastructure & evaluation

  • Datasets and domain-specialized models
  • Simulators and benchmarks
  • Correctness checking
  • Reproducible agentic workflows
  • Evaluation of agent effectiveness and experimentation cost

Review and publication

  • Review is single-blind. Do not anonymize submissions; include author names and affiliations.
  • Reviewers evaluate submissions for relevance, technical substance, and potential to stimulate discussion.
  • Use the HPCA 2027 paper template (download LaTeX).
  • Selected contributions are presented at the workshop.
  • The workshop is nonarchival. Authors are free to publish extended versions elsewhere. With author permission, accepted papers and slides are posted on this website.
